## A method that fills our biggest gap: the dummy Instagram account

From video 2, free and genuinely clever:

1. Create a **brand-new Instagram account**.
2. Search hashtags: **#homefinds, #amazonfinds** and similar.
3. Watch, like and share the Reels that are clearly dropshipping products.
4. The algorithm starts feeding viral dropshipping products continuously.

This is the momentum source `RESEARCH-METHOD.md` was missing. Amazon
Movers & Shakers does not exist for India and we refuse to buy a spy tool,
so this is the free substitute — and it is arguably *better*, because it
surfaces what is going viral on **the exact channel we will advertise on**,
not what is commoditised on a marketplace.

His second free method is Meta Ad Library, which we already use. The third
is a paid spy tool, which we are not buying.

## Partial correction to my own "Amazon is a filter, not a source"

Video 1's whole product research stage is **Amazon Best Sellers** — but
with a filter I had not applied:

- Scroll **deep** into a category, not just the top 30.
- **Skip every branded product** (his example: Huggies). Brands make and
  ship their own goods; they cannot be dropshipped.
- Use Amazon's "**N bought in past month**" figure as demand evidence.
- Collect survivors into a sheet he calls the **Launch Rolodex**: product,
  Amazon link, supplier link, cost price, selling multiple.

My earlier conclusion came from reading the *top* of Home & Kitchen, which
is exactly where the brands and commodity staples sit (Wakefit, Philips,
garbage bags). The non-branded long tail is a different population.

**Revised position:** Amazon is both. A source of non-branded product ideas
when you scroll past the brands, *and* the price-comparison check
afterwards. It is not the enemy I made it out to be — but his model also
accepts a large markup over Amazon pricing, which is the same point the
owner made.

## Five RTO tactics, and one validates our price floor

From video 1, 30:40 onward:

1. **Sell low-return categories**: home & kitchen, gadgets, fitness, pets,
   car accessories, beauty tools. Lowest industry RTO. *(Our niche is on
   this list.)*
2. **Price between Rs 500 and Rs 1,500.** Cheap enough to buy on impulse,
   dear enough that the buyer takes it seriously and will wait for
   delivery. *(Our Rs 1,099 floor sits inside this band — independent
   support for it.)*
3. **Problem-solving in 15 seconds.** The customer must see the problem
   solved within the first seconds of the video. His example: a lizard
   spray — spray the wall, the lizard drops.
4. **Gate COD by customer risk history.** Apps that know whether a given
   customer accepts or refuses parcels; show COD only to good-history
   customers, force everyone else to prepay. He names Pragma and what
   sounds like GoKwik. *(This is Razorpay Magic Checkout's RTO
   Intelligence, already in `AUTOMATION.md`.)*
5. **Charge a COD fee, ~Rs 50**, to nudge people to prepay. A lighter
   version of the Rs 99 partial COD we modelled in `PLAN.md` §2b.

## Real numbers from the Rs 1,000 experiment (video 2)

| | |
|---|---|
| Ad budget | **Rs 300/day per product** |
| Total ad spend | Rs 2,500 over ~4 days |
| Winning product | **fan cleaner**; a magnetic phone mount also sold |
| Order value | ~Rs 1,000-1,200 |
| First-day result | 4-5 sales on ~Rs 1,000 spend → **CPP roughly Rs 200-250** |
| Claimed profit | ~Rs 4,000 |

That CPP is a useful calibration: **Rs 200-250 against our Rs 1,099
break-even CPP of Rs 416 and target of Rs 291.** Comfortably inside. It is
the first external evidence that our price floor is survivable.

## The trap in that video, stated by the presenter himself

> "We've decided to stop running ads, because we want to see how many of
> these orders **actually get delivered**."

**His Rs 4,000 "profit" is pre-RTO.** At 30-40% RTO most of it disappears.
He is honest enough to flag it and the video ends before the answer comes.

This is precisely the failure `PLAN.md` §3 is built to prevent: judging a
product on orders placed rather than orders delivered. Worth remembering
whenever a case study shows a fast profit — almost none of them wait for
the delivery data.

Also relevant: the presenter lost Rs 10,000 on dropshipping a year earlier.
These videos show the attempt that worked, not the ones that did not.
