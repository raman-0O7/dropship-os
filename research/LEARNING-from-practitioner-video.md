· 3.04m views · read via transcript (Hindi), not watched.

**Bias warning, read this first.** The video is (a) an affiliate placement
for the spy tool **Minea** — he gives a 50% coupon code — and (b) a funnel

The *method* is still worth having, and where it matches the independent

## The core method — matches what we already had

> "Sell products that have already proven themselves for someone else."

His reasoning is better than the usual version of it: the dropshipping
product pool is small (he estimates ~10,000 products), and competitors use
the same suppliers you will. So if a product already works for 100 people,
your odds are better than testing something nobody has sold. He is explicit
that it is not a guarantee — you only learn on launch — but it beats
guessing.

This is the same conclusion as the Koala and Yakkyofy guides, reached
independently. **Confirms `RESEARCH-METHOD.md` Stage 2.**

## New and genuinely useful

### 1. The Europe-to-India lag

Sort a spy tool by Western ads that are *about to scale* — proven but not
yet scaled, which means profitable and ramping. Then bring them to India
early.

> "Every product that sells abroad eventually comes to India."

He gives car seat covers as an example. **This is a real edge and we can
approximate it free**: Meta Ad Library with a non-India country filter,
looking for recently-launched ads already running multiple creative
versions. Weaker than Minea's "to scale" sort, but the same idea.

### 2. Supplier strategy is staged

- **Test on a dropship platform.** He names **Roposo Clout** as the best
  Indian option and says explicitly it is not sponsored. Worth evaluating
  against DeoDap, which we picked without comparison.
- **Once a product wins, move to a private supplier** for lower RTO, better
  delivery rate, better quality and a better price.

That staging is sensible: platform convenience while testing, margin and
control once something works.

### 3. Ad structure, concrete

- One sales campaign, **10 ad sets** inside it, each a different
  audience/age/gender/interest.
- **Target the problem, not the demographic.** For a massager: people with
  back pain, cervical pain, headaches. "Different ways to sell the same
  product" — one problem per video ad.
- **7-10 days** to judge a test.
- If ~3 of 10 ad sets are profitable, duplicate those and add interests.
- Then wait for *delivery* data before judging: 100 orders, 50 delivered →
  that is your real revenue, and it sets your maximum acquisition cost.

The last point is the same delivered-margin discipline as `PLAN.md` §3.

### 4. Realistic margin benchmark

**15-30% net profit margin, typically 15-20%.** A useful reality check on
what "working" looks like.

## The correction we should actually absorb

He says RTO control is real but limited:

> Calling every customer, cancelling fake addresses, WhatsApp updates →
> **50% RTO becomes maybe 40%.** About 10 percentage points. "I'm telling
> you averages."

**`AUTOMATION.md` assumes better than that.** Our model has confirmed
orders at 15% RTO against a 30% baseline, and partial COD at 10%. That is a
far bigger improvement than a practitioner reports from calling and
messaging.

Partial COD is structurally different — money paid upfront is a real
commitment, not just a confirmation — so a bigger drop there is defensible.
But the confirmation-only numbers should be treated as optimistic until we
have our own data. **Re-run the COD policy model at a 10-point improvement
and see whether the conclusions still hold.**

## A claim of his that our numbers do not support

> "Focus more on cutting customer acquisition cost than on RTO."

Tested on our Rs 1,099 product, COGS Rs 330 [computed]:

| Lever | Worth per order |
|---|---|
| Cut RTO 30% → 20% | **Rs 58** |
| Cut CPP 20%, Rs 300 → Rs 240 | **Rs 60** |

They are effectively equal. He is right that CAC matters — he is not right
that it dominates. At our price point both levers are worth the same, so we
should not deprioritise RTO work on his advice.

## On his tool recommendation

Minea is $49+/month, roughly Rs 4,000 — a third of our entire budget, and
`RESEARCH-METHOD.md` already rules out paid spy tools. His whole workflow
depends on it, and he is paid to recommend it.

What we lose by not buying it: the "sort by about-to-scale", the
auto-updating "success radar" of currently-exploding products, and the
creative finder. Meta Ad Library covers the core — who is advertising what,
and for how long — but not the ranking. That is a real gap, and the honest
mitigation is more manual searching, not pretending the free tool is equal.

Revisit only if a product becomes profitable and the tool can be paid for
out of its margin.
