# Product Research Method

Built from published methodology, not instinct. Sources at the bottom.
Most of what is written about dropshipping product research is vendor
content selling a tool, and nearly all of it assumes a US/EU buyer with a
$100 cart and 2-day delivery. Where a borrowed rule does not survive
contact with the Indian market, it is marked and replaced.

**Order of work: demand first, catalog last.** Looking at DeoDap's catalog
before knowing what is in demand anchors research on what one supplier
happens to stock. Decide what to sell, then find who stocks it.

---

## Stage 0 — Timing (do this before anything else)

Ad cost is seasonal and it moves the break-even math more than most
product decisions do. Indian festive CPMs run **30-60% above baseline**
[source: Criteo, paceads].

For a Rs 899 product (break-even CPP Rs 342, target Rs 239):

| Period | CPC | CVR | CPP | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline months | Rs 6.0 | 2.0% | Rs 300 | breaks even |
| Festive peak, CPM +40% | Rs 8.4 | 2.0% | Rs 420 | **loses money** |
| Festive peak, CPM +60% | Rs 9.6 | 2.0% | Rs 480 | **loses money** |
| January trough, CPM -15% | Rs 5.1 | 2.0% | Rs 255 | breaks even |

[computed; CPC range Rs 4-20 source: upGrowth]

**The same product is profitable in January and loss-making in late
October.** A small budget testing during peak season pays a 40% premium
for identical learning. Test in the trough; scale into the peak.

### Calendar that matters for home decor

## Stage 1 — Demand signal (free sources only)

**Corrected 2026-10-12: hunt momentum, not stability.** Earlier versions of
this file carried the US-guide rule that "flat or gently rising is healthy,
a curve past its peak is a reject". That advice is written for organic
search and brand building. **We sell through paid Meta/Instagram ads, which
is discovery, not search.** Nobody searches for our product; they get shown
it. Two consequences:

- Google Trends measures *search intent*, so it is a **secondary** signal
  here — useful for ruling out a dead fad, not for picking a winner.
- A product selling at a low constant rate for six months cannot produce
  the fast win a Rs 15,000 capped experiment needs. **We want what is
  spiking now**, and small and light, so shipping does not eat the margin.

Ranking is therefore by current momentum: Trends "Breakout" rising
queries, and recently-launched ads that are clearly scaling. Multi-year
stability is a tiebreaker, not a filter.

**And the product must not be price-comparable.** Paid social sells "I did
not know I wanted this". If a buyer can find the same item on Meesho for a
fifth of our price in one search, no creative or pricing strategy saves it.

**Hard rule: no branded products.** We have no right to resell another
brand. Anything carrying a brand name is dropped at the filter stage
without scoring.

Four free sources, in this order. None of them costs money and together
they replace a Rs 3,300/month spy tool.

**1. The dummy Instagram account — PRIMARY momentum source.**
From `research/LEARNING-videos-2.md`. Amazon Movers & Shakers does not
exist for India and we are not buying a spy tool, so this is how we find
what is spiking:

1. Create a **brand-new Instagram account** (clean algorithm).
2. Search hashtags: **#homefinds, #amazonfinds**, and niche variants.
3. Watch, like and share Reels that are obviously dropshipping products.
4. The feed then serves viral dropshipping products continuously.

It is better than a marketplace list for our purpose, because it shows
what is going viral on **the exact channel we will advertise on**.

**2. Amazon India Best Sellers — a source too, if filtered properly.**
Scroll **deep** past the top of a category; the top is where brands and
commodity staples sit. **Skip every branded product** — brands make and
ship their own goods and cannot be dropshipped. Use the "**N bought in
past month**" figure as demand evidence. Then still run the undercut check
below before committing.

**3. Google Trends, region India.** Two views per term: 90-day for
momentum, 5-year to tell a trend from a dying fad. Flat or gently rising
is healthy; a curve past its peak is a reject [source: Koala, Yakkyofy].

**4. Amazon / Flipkart / Meesho as the undercut CHECK.**

The *top* of any Amazon category is brands and commodity staples, and
taking ideas from there leads into a fight we cannot win on price or
delivery speed. That was the original warning and it still holds for the
top of the list — but the non-branded long tail is a legitimate source
(see 2 above).

Correct use as a check: take a candidate found elsewhere and search it on Amazon,
Flipkart and Meesho — then ask the right question. **Not** "is it cheaper
there" (it always is), but:

> Is the **identical** item there at a large multiple cheaper, with fast
> delivery and heavy social proof, and do we have no differentiation story?

Marketplace price does not cap our price; we price as we see fit. What a
visible cheaper alternative does is **tax our conversion rate**, and since
CPP = CPC / CVR, conversion is what decides survival. At Rs 1,099 we need
1.4-2.4% CVR against a normal Indian band of 1-3% [computed] — tight but
inside it.

So a big gap on a differentiated product is a creative problem. A big gap
on an identical commodity with 20,000 reviews is a kill. Run this BEFORE
any creative work.

(Note: Amazon India does not populate Movers & Shakers for Home & Kitchen —
it returns "no movers and shakers available in this category". The guide
recommending it described Amazon US.)

**5. Meesho and Flipkart trending.** Meesho matters disproportionately
here: its buyers are tier-2/3 women, which is close to our target. No
public API gives a verifiable trending list [source: ecomadvisors], so
this is manual browsing, treated as leads not proof.

**6. Pinterest Trends.** Home decor is a Pinterest-native category and
Pinterest leads purchase intent by weeks. Underused by Indian
dropshippers, which makes it more valuable, not less.

**On TikTok Creative Center:** every guide recommends it. TikTok is banned
in India, so it says nothing about Indian demand. Useful only as a global
leading indicator of a format or product that may arrive here later.
Instagram Reels is the actual channel.

## Stage 2 — Proof someone is already profiting

Meta Ad Library, free, India filter, no login.

**The key signal is ad age, not advertiser count.** An ad running six
months or longer is very likely profitable — nobody funds a loser for six
months [source: Koala]. Finding a long-running competitor is the single
strongest free validation available.

- Ads 6+ months old → **proven demand. Good sign.**
- Every ad under 4 weeks old → unproven. Wait and watch [source: Koala].
- Many advertisers, all running the *same* creative angle → the angle is
  used up. Need a different angle, not a different product.
- Many advertisers undercutting on price with faster delivery → reject.

Record the ad's hook, its first frame, and its offer. Those are reusable;
the product alone is not.

## Stage 2b — The Europe-to-India lag

From a practitioner source, `research/LEARNING-from-practitioner-video.md`:
products that sell in Europe and the US arrive in India later. So a product
that is *proven abroad but not yet common here* is pre-validated demand
with less local competition.

Free approximation of the paid-tool version: Meta Ad Library with a
non-India country filter (UK, US, Australia), looking for ads launched in
the last 1-3 months that already run **multiple creative versions** — that
combination signals a scaling spend, which signals profitability.

Then check whether anyone is running it in India yet. Proven abroad, absent
here, and sourceable from an Indian supplier is the best shape we can find.

## Stage 3 — The Indian reality check

This stage has no equivalent in the Western guides and is where most of
our edge sits.

- **Marketplace undercut test.** Search the item on Amazon India, Flipkart
  and Meesho. If it is there cheaper with 2-day delivery, our 5-7 day
  shipping loses. This kills a large share of otherwise good candidates
  and must be checked *before* any creative work.
- **COD survivability.** Would a buyer refuse this at the door? Impulse
  decor under Rs 500 has the worst RTO. Anything that looks cheaper in
  person than in the ad is an RTO machine.
- **Fragility.** No glass, ceramic, resin, marble. Two courier legs.
- **Bundle path to Rs 899.** See `PLAN.md` §2.

## Stage 3b — RTO is partly a product-choice problem

Five tactics from a practitioner [source: `research/LEARNING-videos-2.md`],
two of which are selection criteria rather than operations:

1. **Low-return categories**: home & kitchen, gadgets, fitness, pets, car
   accessories, beauty tools. Our niche is on this list.
2. **Price Rs 500-1,500.** Cheap enough to buy on impulse, dear enough that
   the buyer takes delivery seriously. Independent support for our
   Rs 1,099 floor.
3. **Problem solved within 15 seconds** of the video.
4. Gate COD by customer risk history (Razorpay RTO Intelligence, GoKwik).
5. Charge a COD fee ~Rs 50 to nudge prepaid.

## Stage 4 — Margin, computed not guessed

```
python3 tools/unit_economics.py --sp <price> --cogs <cost> --sensitivity
python3 tools/score_products.py data/catalog/<export>.csv
```

Borrowed rules of thumb and whether they survive here:

| Guide rule | Source | Verdict for India |
|---|---|---|
| Price above $100 | Koala | **No.** Indian decor AOV is Rs 500-1,500 |
| 3x landed cost minimum | Koala, Yakkyofy | **Yes**, and our Rs 899 floor encodes it |
| 50-60% gross margin | Koala | **Yes**, roughly what Rs 899/Rs 280 gives |
| CAC floor $20-30 | Koala | **No.** India CPP is Rs 150-400 |
| Returns allowance 3-5% | Koala | **No.** COD RTO is 20-40%. Ten times off |
| Ads 6+ months = profitable | Koala | **Yes.** Transfers directly |
| 2-5 day delivery expected | Koala | **No.** 5-7 days is normal here |

The two that do not transfer — returns allowance and CAC floor — are
exactly the two that bankrupt people who copy a US playbook.

## Stage 4b — Ad structure, for when we get there

From the same practitioner source, recorded here so it is not re-derived:

- One sales campaign, **10 ad sets**, each a different interest/audience.
- **Target the problem, not the demographic.** One problem per video ad.
- **7-10 days** to judge a test.
- If ~3 of 10 ad sets are profitable, duplicate those and add interests.
- Judge on **delivered** orders, not placed orders — that sets the real
  maximum acquisition cost. Same discipline as `PLAN.md` §3.
- Realistic net margin benchmark: **15-20%**.
- Test budget observed in a real low-budget run: **Rs 300/day per product**.
- Observed CPP in that run: **Rs 200-250** at a Rs 1,000-1,200 order value,
  against our Rs 1,099 break-even CPP of Rs 416. First external evidence
  our floor is survivable.
- **Judge on delivered orders.** That run reported ~Rs 4,000 profit and then
  admitted it had stopped ads to find out how many would actually deliver.
  Nearly every case study you will see is quoting pre-RTO numbers.

## Verdict on paid tools

Minea, Dropship.io, Sell The Trend, PiPiAds and similar run **$39-49/month,
roughly Rs 3,300-4,200** [source: Koala, zikanalytics, outfy]. One month of
one tool is a third of the entire Rs 15,000 budget, and they are mostly
indexes of Western ads. **Recommendation: buy none.** The free stack above
plus manual browser work covers the same ground. Revisit only once a
product is profitable and the tool would be paid for from its margin.
